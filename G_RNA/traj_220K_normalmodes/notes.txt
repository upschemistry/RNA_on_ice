OK, here's a methodology (for getting the C=O stretch)

1. For the undriven system, isolate (calculate) the C=O stretch coordinate, find its power spectrum, and look for the highest peak. This occurs at 56.2 ps-1 (1873 cm-1). The RMS motion of C=O is 4.1%. Examination of the covariance modes shows that mode #90 is the C=O stretch -- a very clean spectrum at 56.2 as a matter of fact.

2. Drive with an EM pulse at that frequency to amplify the mode, and check the C=O stretch coordinate's RMS deviation. If it's bigger, it's an indication that it's the C=O stretch mode. In the case of 56.2 ps-1 (1873 cm-1), the driven RMS motion is bigger than in the undriven system, at 5.8%, so that's a positive indicator. To check this conclusion, we can do the covariance analysis and look around for a mode that has a high amplitude in the C=O stretch at that frequency. Examination of those modes indicates that modes #90 and 91 seem to have a large contribution from the C=O stretch.

Conclusion: 1873 cm-1 is almost surely the C=O stretch.
